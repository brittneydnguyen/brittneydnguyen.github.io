Learning is like making a good soup.

One of my favorite Vietnamese dishes is bún bò huế, a spicy beef and pork soup. Its primary ingredients are onions, lemongrass, shrimp paste, fish sauce, and—of course—lots of beef and pork cuts, all simmered together in a big pot for quite some time.

When you eat bún bò huế, you're not necessarily tasting each of the individual components. Sure, some bits are more prominents than others: you might accidentally bite into a chunk of lemongrass, and you will fiend at the sight of a tender oxtail in your bowl. But you couldn't tell me that each bowl contains a teaspoon of fish sauce and 0.2783 of an onion. All of the flavors are married together in this rich and savory broth, such that together, they are greater than the sum of their parts.

As an educator, I focus on the whole student. The capitalist society that seeks to optimize production trains students to think myopically, focusing solely on an end goal. However, as we learn, we go through all sorts of experiences that affect our personhood! This final exhibition & reflection activity is designed for students to identify how their experiences season their intellectual and emotional identities, while also showing off the skills they learned in class. Students also played a direct role in the creation of this project by answering a survey, which constituted the deidentified data for this activity!

In addition, I believe that humor plays a huge part in keeping students engaged, which is why I taught students joins in R using hypothetical hamsters.
